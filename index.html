Validate it on you

Faulkner et al. (2024) found that the same 1–3 cm change made some riders faster and others slower. Treat every number here as a starting point and test it.

  1. Change one thing at a time. Saddle height first, then setback, then front end.
  2. Film yourself side-on at race effort. Check the knee bend at the bottom of the stroke falls in your target range.
  3. Ride 2–3 weeks before judging comfort. Studies show trained riders adapt to aero positions.
  4. Measure: same course, same power, compare speed. An aero sensor or velodrome laps give CdA directly.
  5. Keep the change only if speed improves without new pain.

This configurator gives research-informed starting points, not a professional bike fit. The saddle-height formula (LeMond, 0.883 × inseam) and the mm-per-degree conversion are approximations: verify the actual knee angle on video. The brand and size are used to label your sheet; this page does not hold manufacturers' geometry, so read the seat tube angle from your brand's official chart. If you have pain, see a qualified bike fitter or physiotherapist. The colour themes are inspired by the leader's jerseys of cycling's three Grand Tours; this tool is not affiliated with or endorsed by their organisers.
